Game "Review" - Stick it to the Stickman

I try to play games whenever I can make the time for it, so I thought I would lay out my thoughts on this game I've been recently playing while I'm making this page.

Gameplay

Combat

The game is a simple physics based rougelite brawler that you can control mostly with one button. Despite how simple it is, I think at least half of the fun comes from seeing your coworkers (the enemies of the game) get flung around like ragdolls. Otherwise, the attacks you use are in a fixed order that are added onto when you gain them from leveling up or random drops. You can also upgrade them to make them stronger, but that's the most complex it gets.

Because of this, the randomness that you would expect from a rougelite isn't as impactful as you'd expect. The skill expression you can have in the game is similar to how you'd see it with most other moves. Barring some exceptions and their upgraded forms, a lot of moves are similarly strong. The decisions on what moves to pick will start boiling down to which martial arts/silly miscellaneous attacks you think are cooler. This isn't a problem for me, because I think a lot of the mileage you get out of this game comes from how amusing you think the combat is, or the humor, which I'll cover in the next major section.

Progression

As a rougelite, you're able to make some sort of progress between each attempt of the game you go through. In this game, that's in the form of paying (not with real money) for more features within the level and unlocking new modes. Each of the modes have their own separate progression, plus their own gimmicks. Most of the modes are similar in gameplay style except for two that I won't spoil. As if reading this will make you want to play the game.

Progress between these modes are intertwined, because some upgrades are blocked by progress in other modes. It encourages players to play multiple modes, but depending on how you look at it, you could say the game forces you. I'm fine with this too, since multiple modes help the game get less repetitive from its simplicity.

Comedy

The other main draw of this game is its comedy. As you can guess from the premise of stickmen beating each other up, it's pretty crude. Although, it's not exactly for small children on account of the violence, even if extremely cartoony, and the subject matter. The game is a hyper satirical parody of capitalism in the modern era. The whole thing is themed around corporations and how comically evil they can get. Upgrades are called promotions, your enemies are your coworkers, and a lot of features and jokes are explicit referencing real life companies and their practices/scandals.

It's very tongue in cheek, and how much you enjoy the game is going to be heavily reliant on if you find its form of comedy funny. Naturally, you have to have enough of an understanding about the world to even get these jokes, so it's targeting a teen to young adult age range in its demographics. I'm not sure how much older people will end up enjoying it, but I do to a degree. I don't remember laughing out loud at much other than me launching coworkers into the stratosphere, but I do think the jokes are fairly funny. It's the other piece of the puzzle that makes the game, so if someone were to not find it funny, they should probably save their money.
